## Analytical Frameworks

### Shaheed Balancing Test (Search Warrant Validity)
Apply to every search/seizure mentioned in disclosure:

1. **Was there a search/seizure?** (s21 NZBORA engaged)
2. **Was it authorised?** (warrant, statutory power, consent)
3. **If warrant:** Was it validly issued? (reasonable grounds, particularity, proper issuing officer)
4. **If warrantless:** Did a statutory exception apply? Was it reasonable?
5. **If breach occurred:**
   - Seriousness of the breach
   - Whether deliberate or reckless
   - Availability of other remedies
   - Would admission undermine s4 Evidence Act (fairness/rights)
   → **s56 Evidence Act exclusion analysis**

### Section 56 Evidence Act Exclusion Analysis
For every piece of evidence obtained through search, interview, or seizure:

**Step 1:** Was the evidence obtained unfairly? (breach of NZBORA, improper procedure, oppression, false representation)
**Step 2:** If yes, would admission undermine s4 purpose? (fairness to parties, rights recognition)
**Step 3:** Consider s56(2) factors:
- (a) Circumstances in which evidence obtained
- (b) Nature and significance of breach
- (c) Nature and seriousness of offence
- (d) Nature and significance of right breached
- (e) Whether reasonable alternatives to breach available
- (f) Whether evidence could have been obtained lawfully
- (g) Nature and seriousness of exigency
- (h) Other relevant matters

### Charge Element Deconstruction
For each charge, deconstruct using:

1. **Statutory offence section** — identify each element from the Crimes Act or other statute
2. **Actus reus** — what physical act/conduct must be proven
3. **Mens rea** — what mental element (intent, knowledge, recklessness, negligence)
4. **Circumstance elements** — any required circumstances (location, time, relationship)
5. **Causation** — any required link between act and result
6. **Defences available** — statutory and common law defences that may apply
7. **Evidential sufficiency** — is there evidence for each element?
8. **Credibility assessment** — strengths/weaknesses of Crown evidence

## Disclosure Obligations (CPA 2011 Detail)

### Initial Disclosure (s60) — Must provide without request:
- (a) Copy of charging document
- (b) Summary of facts
- (c) Defendant's criminal record (if any)
- (d) Any expert report Crown intends to rely on
- (e) Any video/audio recording Crown intends to rely on
- (f) Any written statement of a witness Crown intends to call
- (g) Any other information that may assist defence

### Full Disclosure (s61) — Must provide:
All relevant material that the prosecutor has or controls, including:
- Material that could undermine Crown case
- Material that could assist defence
- Material relevant to reliability of Crown evidence
- Material relevant to credibility of Crown witnesses
- Material that may affect sentence

### Continuing Obligation (s62):
If prosecutor obtains or becomes aware of relevant material after initial/full disclosure, must disclose as soon as practicable.

### Exceptions (s63):
May withhold material if:
- Public interest in non-disclosure outweighs interest in disclosure
- Must give reasons for withholding
- Must notify court
- Court may review withholding decision

### Timing (s64):
- Initial disclosure: as soon as practicable after filing charge
- Full disclosure: no later than commencement of case review
- Evidential disclosure: no later than 5 working days before trial/hearing

### Defence Disclosure (s65-67):
- Defence not required to disclose, but if alibi or expert evidence intended, must give notice
- Failure to give alibi notice → court may draw adverse inference
- Expert evidence without notice → may require adjournment or exclude

## Offence Element Structure

### Common Offence Elements (quick reference for deconstruction)

#### Assault (s196 Crimes Act 1961)
- **Actus reus:** Direct or indirect application of force to another person (without consent)
- **Mens rea:** Intentional or reckless (s24 applies)
- **Defences:** Self-defence (s48), consent (s59 sporting/domestic), lawful authority

#### Male Assaults Female (s194 Crimes Act 1961)
- **Actus reus:** Assaults any female (direct/indirect force)
- **Mens rea:** Intentional
- **Circumstance:** Victim is female; accused is male

#### Injures with Intent (s189(1) Crimes Act 1961)
- **Actus reus:** Wounds, maims, disfigures, or causes grievous bodily harm
- **Mens rea:** Intentionally (with intent to cause the specific harm)
- **Causation:** Direct causal link between act and injury

#### Theft (s219 Crimes Act 1961)
- **Actus reus:** Takes or obtains property capable of being stolen
- **Mens rea:** Dishonestly and without claim of right; with intent to deprive owner permanently
- **Circumstance:** Property belongs to another

#### Burglary (s231 Crimes Act 1961)
- **Actus reus:** Enters any building or ship (or part thereof)
- **Mens rea:** With intent to commit an imprisonable offence in the building
- **Circumstance:** Building/ship (definition includes temporary structures)

#### Unlawful Taking of Motor Vehicle (s73 Summary Offences Act 1981 / s242 Crimes Act 1961)
- **Actus reus:** Takes or uses motor vehicle
- **Mens rea:** Without consent of owner/person in lawful possession; knows or believes no consent
- **Circumstance:** Vehicle is motor vehicle as defined

#### Possession of offensive weapon (s202A Crimes Act 1961)
- **Actus reus:** Has possession of offensive weapon or knife
- **Mens rea:** Without lawful authority or reasonable excuse
- **Circumstance:** In any public place
- **Definitions:** Offensive weapon = made/adapted/intended for causing bodily injury

#### Drugs — possession (s6 Misuse of Drugs Act 1975)
- **Actus reus:** Has possession of controlled drug
- **Mens rea:** Knowingly (knows substance is present and knows nature of substance)
- **Circumstance:** Substance is controlled drug (schedule-dependent class)

#### Drugs — supply/dealing (s6 Misuse of Drugs Act 1975)
- **Actus reus:** Supplies/administers/gives or offers to supply; or possesses for supply
- **Mens rea:** Intentionally
- **Circumstance:** Controlled drug; supply = transfer of physical control

#### Threatening to Kill / Do GBH (s306 Crimes Act 1961)
- **Actus reus:** Threatens to kill or do grievous bodily harm
- **Mens rea:** Intentionally or recklessly (knows threat may be taken seriously)
- **Circumstance:** Threat communicated to person or their property

#### Wilful Damage (s269 Crimes Act 1961)
- **Actus reus:** Damages/destroys property
- **Mens rea:** Intentionally or recklessly; without claim of right or excuse
